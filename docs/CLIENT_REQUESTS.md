# What CRASA needs to supply

**CRASA Digital Anchor Ecosystem**
Contract ZM-COMESA-CRASA/WEBSITE/25/07/2025-as
Prepared 9 September 2026

---

## How to read this list

The platform is built. Everything below is content, confirmation or
permission that only CRASA can give, and each item is a place where the
software is finished and waiting.

**Ordered by how much it blocks, not by category.** The first four items each
hold up something visible on the day of the validation workshop. The last few
can follow afterwards without anything looking unfinished.

Against each item we have said roughly how much is needed, so nobody has to
guess whether we want a sentence or a page.

Where CRASA has nothing to give us yet, tell us that and we will show a
designed holding line rather than a blank space. **Nothing here is a
blocker in the sense of stopping the build.** These are the places where the
platform is currently telling a visitor that content is being prepared.

# Blocking the most

## 1. Photography

**Why it blocks the most.** It is the first thing anyone sees, it is on the
page CRASA will open in front of the workshop, and it cannot be worked around
with a placeholder that does not look like a placeholder.

### What the homepage does today

**The banner carries a photograph again, since 9 September 2026.** Delegates
around a glass boardroom table, one taking notes, from the second batch you
supplied. It is the first genuinely institutional image in the library and it
is the right register: a formal meeting of people who look like they decide
things.

It took three attempts to get there, and the two that failed are worth
recording because they explain what the slot needs. We tried a market scene
in Cape Town, and then a telecommunications equipment bench. The second is at
least a regulatory activity and it is still not what CRASA is. Both were
withdrawn, and the banner ran on navy and typography alone for a day rather
than carry either.

A banner photograph is a claim about an organisation, made on the first
screen, to a reader who is usually a regulator deciding whether to trust the
rest of the page. A stranger shopping, or a laboratory bench, says CRASA is a
consumer body or a testing house. Neither is true, and the wrong claim costs
more than the empty one.

**This does not close the request below.** One photograph is now carrying the
homepage banner, the About page, the Committees page and the Events page,
because it is the only institutional image that exists. Three pages sharing a
picture is thin, and it is stock: a reader who has sat in a CRASA meeting
will not recognise the room.

### One more institutional photograph, for the third banner slide

The banner rotates through three slides and all three now carry a
photograph. The third one is the weakest image in the library — two people
at a laptop in an atrium — and it is there because it is the only remaining
real photograph, not because it belongs.

It would pass the substitution test for any company on earth. On a slide
whose copy is about CRASA's own published library, that is a mismatch a
delegate will feel without being able to name.

**What would replace it:** a plenary session, or the signing of a
determination. The same subjects as the request above, and one more frame of
the same shoot would do it. When it arrives, slide three should be looked at
again rather than left as it is.

### The single highest-value item on this whole list

**CRASA's own photography of its own meetings.** Not the first item because
it is urgent — because nothing purchasable can substitute for it.

The AGM, committee sessions, the signing of a determination, the Secretariat
at work, an Executive Committee meeting in progress. Any of it. Even
phone-camera photographs from a delegate, if they are sharp and the room is
recognisable, are worth more than the best stock image money can buy, for
two reasons that have nothing to do with quality:

1. They are unarguably CRASA. A reader who has sat in that room recognises
   it, and nobody can say the site could belong to any regional body.
2. They cannot be wrong about what CRASA is, because they are a record of
   what CRASA did.

If there is an archive of AGM photographs anywhere — a Secretariat drive, a
communications officer's folder, a past event report — that archive is the
answer to this entire section. Please send it before anything is
commissioned or bought.

### The register we are asking for

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Subject | Delegates in plenary. A signing ceremony. A speaker at a podium with member state flags. Institutional architecture — a regulator's building, a headquarters, a conference venue. Telecommunications infrastructure at scale: a landing station, a tower array, a transmission site. A network operations centre |
| Register | Institutional. The photograph should look like a record of an event rather than an advertisement for one |
| Orientation | Landscape |
| Size | At least 3000 pixels on the long edge |
| Composition | **The subject and the visual interest must sit in the right-hand two thirds.** The headline sits over the left third, and an image with its subject on the left puts type over a face |
| How many | Three for the banner, which rotates. One image shown three times is not a rotation; with one image we show one image and no controls. One good photograph is worth more than three weak ones, so send what exists rather than making up the number |

### What it must not be

These are not stylistic preferences. Each one has been tried on this platform
or on a comparable one and reads wrong to the intended audience.

- **People using phones.** A hand holding a handset is a photograph about
  consumers. CRASA regulates the operators, not the users, and a consumer
  image on the front page misstates the mandate to the ministries reading it.
- **Market scenes, street scenes, shopfronts.** Same reason. This was the
  banner until 9 September 2026 and it was withdrawn for exactly this.
- **Smiling stock subjects.** A person photographed to look approachable is
  photographed to sell something. Nothing on this platform is for sale.
- **Generic corporate stock.** Handshakes over a boardroom table, a team
  pointing at a screen, suited figures against glass towers.
- **Anything AI-generated.** Flawless skin, symmetrical faces, glassy eyes,
  impossibly tidy offices. On a regulatory platform this destroys credibility
  faster than a poor photograph does, because it makes a reader wonder what
  else was generated.
- **Anything visibly not Southern African.** Delegates from fourteen SADC
  regulators will notice within a second.
- **Gradients or abstract shapes standing in for a photograph.** If there is
  no photograph, the band carries type on navy and says so by looking
  composed. It does not pretend.

### Elsewhere on the site

| Where | What | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Member states page | Optional: a landmark or regulator building per state | Up to 14, landscape, 1600px+ |
| Newsroom | One image per news item | As articles are written |
| About page | The Secretariat building or a meeting in progress | 2 to 3 |

### Licensing

Every image needs to be either CRASA's own or cleared for publication. The
platform records the rights holder, the licence and a confirmation date
against each image, so that a question from COMESA can be answered in
minutes. We will need that information as the images arrive.

## 2. Logos and marks

**Why it blocks.** Fourteen member states currently appear without their
authority's mark, and CRASA's own logo is a working copy rather than the
official file.

### CRASA's own

| Item | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary logo | SVG (vector) | The official file, not an export from a document |
| Single-colour white version | SVG | For the navy footer and dark surfaces |
| Favicon set | 32px, 180px, 512px PNG | The small icon in a browser tab |

### The fourteen national authorities

One mark per authority. **SVG strongly preferred**; failing that, PNG at
**512 pixels minimum on the long edge with a transparent background**.

One of the sample marks currently in the archive is 412 by 118 pixels on a
white rectangle, which cannot be used on any coloured surface and cannot be
enlarged. That is the standard to avoid.

We can request these from the authorities directly if CRASA prefers, but the
request will carry more weight from the Secretariat.

# Blocking a visible page

## 5. The About page: four sections

Each is a section of the About page that currently shows a holding line. The
lengths below are what the layout is built for.

### 5a. Governance

**About 150 to 250 words.** How the Annual General Meeting, the Executive
Committee and the Secretariat relate to one another, how decisions pass
between them, the meeting cycle, and who currently holds each office.

### 5b. The Executive Committee

**About 100 to 200 words.** How many members it has, how they are elected and
for how long, which authorities currently hold the chair and the vice-chair,
and what the committee decides as opposed to what it recommends.

### 5c. The Secretariat

**About 100 to 200 words.** Where it is based, when it was established, the
Executive Secretary and the functional roles, and how to reach each. The
contact page already carries the general address; this is the standing
description.

### 5d. Partnerships

**Length depends entirely on CRASA.** Which organisations CRASA works with
and in what capacity.

**This one is different from the others and we will not draft it even as a
placeholder.** Partner and funder attribution carries binding rules on
wording, on the proportions of an emblem and on the space around it. The
European Union emblem in particular has published rules, and a wrong one on a
donor-funded platform is raised formally. We need the exact wording and the
exact marks from CRASA, and until we have them the section stays empty.

# Blocking a page that must not be empty at go-live

## 8. Thirteen decisions in the privacy notice and the terms of use

**Why it blocks.** Both pages are linked from the footer of every page and
both must be settled before the platform is public.

**What is already done.** Both carry a full draft, written from what the
platform actually does: what it stores, what each cookie is for, that
publication here gives a document no legal force in any member state, and
that a demonstration figure stays badged until its own regulator confirms it.
Neither is a holding line. The accessibility statement beside them is a
statement about how the platform was built, so it is written and complete and
needs nothing from CRASA.

**What is left is thirteen decisions**, each one a legal commitment rather
than a fact about the software. Each appears in the text as a square-bracketed
sentence naming who must supply it, so nothing is invented and nothing is
missing without saying so. A wrong retention period on a published privacy
notice is a compliance failure CRASA would inherit from us.

### Privacy notice — seven

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| 1 | The data controller of record, and whether a data protection officer is appointed and how to reach them |
| 2 | Retention periods. Our recommendation is in the text: enquiries 24 months, audit log for the life of the platform, delegate registrations 12 months after the meeting, accounts until the Secretariat closes them |
| 3 | Whether any data is shared with COMESA, with SADC or with a funder, and on what basis |
| 4 | The hosting location, and whether any data leaves the SADC region, backups included |
| 5 | The rights available to a reader and the law they arise under |
| 6 | The supervisory authority a reader may complain to, and the governing jurisdiction |
| 7 | How changes to the notice will be communicated |

### Terms of use — six

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| 8 | Liability for reliance on the indicator values. Our position is in the text: the figures are reported by member states and CRASA publishes them; it does not warrant them |
| 9 | Which language governs where the English, French and Portuguese differ |
| 10 | The licence under which CRASA's own material may be reused, and whether attribution is required |
| 11 | Any service commitment, or an explicit statement that none is made |
| 12 | How changes to the terms are communicated and from when they take effect |
| 13 | The governing law and the courts having jurisdiction. Not a question the build can answer: CRASA is a Specialised Agency of SADC with fourteen member states |

**What changed on 9 September 2026.** Each page used to open with a paragraph
announcing itself a draft CRASA had not reviewed. That is a fact about this
project rather than a fact about privacy or about terms of use, and it met
every public reader before the text did. It is gone from the public page. The
review state now lives in the back office: the page list carries a client
review column, the dashboard carries a band naming every page still waiting,
and the page editor lists the outstanding decisions above by name. **None of
it appears on the public page.** An administrator clears it in one click,
which stamps who signed it off and when.

**The square brackets themselves are still in the public text**, because they
are the text and removing them would delete content rather than a banner. Say
the word and they come out, either by supplying the thirteen answers or by
telling us to cut the sentences that carry them.

Both pages are editable like every other page: rich text blocks in the page
editor, three languages side by side, draft, review and published states, and
a version history that keeps what the page said rather than only that it
changed.

# Not blocking anything visible

## 10. Single sign-on details

**A separate document, `SSO_REQUIREMENTS.md`, is enclosed.** It is written to
be forwarded to whoever runs the identity platform, and it asks for seven
things: which platform holds the accounts, which protocol it speaks, the
technical endpoints, which attribute identifies a person permanently, whether
that platform may create accounts on this one, two test accounts, and a named
technical contact.

**Nothing is blocked while this is outstanding.** The platform authenticates
against its own accounts today, and that is a complete and delivered answer.
The bridge is added whenever the answers arrive, without downtime and without
anyone having to change their password.

One question in that document has a security consequence and carries our
recommendation: **the identity platform should not be able to create accounts
here.** Otherwise anyone it trusts becomes a user of a platform holding
country-scoped data and confidential committee papers, without anyone at the
Secretariat deciding so.

The events section and the meetings calendar are built and empty. We have

**What we need, per meeting:** title, dates, location, which committee owns

## 12. Regulatory indicator values

The comparator and the completeness dashboard are built and are showing
demonstration data, clearly marked as such on screen and in every export.

**This is the one item that resolves itself through the platform rather than
by sending us a file.** Each member state files its own figures through the
data submission form, the Secretariat validates them, and validated figures
replace the demonstration data automatically. The workshop is the right place
to walk the member states through that.

If CRASA would rather load historic figures in bulk, the platform accepts a
spreadsheet: a template can be downloaded from the submission screen, filled
in and uploaded, and it reports any problems by row and column before writing
anything.

# Added 10 September 2026, after the legacy import

## The annual reports themselves

**What we need:** the annual report PDFs, one per year, with the year each
covers.

**Why we are asking:** the legacy site's Annual Reports category has one
page, and its Download button goes to a SharePoint folder rather than to a
file. We will not carry that link across. The platform's own words are
*"Every download comes from the platform itself. No redirect to Google
Drive or SharePoint anywhere,"* and a treaty body's annual report behind a
personal OneDrive share also stops working the day that account is closed.

**How much:** the files. Nothing else. The category, the filter and the
publication type all exist and are wired; the shelf is empty.

## Twelve publications where the title disagrees with the old category

**What we need:** ten minutes with a list.

**Why we are asking:** the ruling on the import says that where a
guideline's title names a sector and the old category says a different one,
the title wins and the row is flagged. Twelve rows were flagged. Two
examples: *SADC ICT and Broadcasting Infrastructure Sharing Guidelines* and
*Framework on the Establishment of Digital Sound Broadcasting in SADC* were
filed under electronic communications on the old site and are filed under
broadcasting here.

**How much:** confirm or correct. The list is in the back office with the
reason on each row.

## Eighteen items with no date anywhere

**What we need:** the publication dates, where you have them.

**Why we are asking:** eighteen of the imported items state no date on the
old page, carry no edition year in their standfirst and have no year in
their filename. They are dated to the day of the crawl and marked as such.
A regulator citing a guideline needs its year.

## Twenty-eight headlines we re-cased

**What we need:** a read-through, not a decision.

**Why we are asking:** most of the old site's headlines are set in
capitals. The house style is sentence case, so they were re-cased on
import, with acronyms and place names held by a list rather than guessed.
Twenty-eight are flagged so somebody can read them once.

## Twenty-four photographs we would like cleared

**What we need:** confirmation that CRASA holds the rights and that the
people in them may appear on the public site.

**Why we are asking:** twenty-four of the sixty-five photographs from the
old site show CRASA meetings, signings, courtesy calls or delegates. They
are the institutional pictures the platform is short of. Nothing has been
placed on any page: what a picture shows, and whether the faces in it are
cleared, is a judgement for the Secretariat. `docs/LEGACY_PHOTOGRAPHS.md`
lists each one with what it shows and its size.

**Our reading of the two largest:** neither is right for the homepage. One
is a single speaker under a CRAN pull-up banner, and a regional
association whose homepage opens on one member state's branding is making a
claim it does not mean to. The other is a delegation line-up in BEREC's
lobby in Europe beside an EU flag, which is both outside the region and
carries an emblem we may not show until CRASA supplies the wording that
goes with it.

## Permission to show the fourteen members' own logos

**What we need:** confirmation that CRASA may publish its member states'
logos on this platform, and that the member states are content for it to.

**Why we are asking:** the fourteen national regulators' marks are now on
the members page and the member grid. They came from CRASA's own About
page, where CRASA has been publishing them for years. That is where the
question sits: CRASA publishing a member's mark on crasa.org is not
automatically the same permission as this platform publishing it, and these
are fourteen registered trademarks belonging to fourteen other
organisations, several of them state bodies with their own brand rules.

CLAUDE.md already holds one carve-out on exactly this reasoning, for the
four social network marks, on the grounds that a recoloured brand mark is a
guideline breach and on a treaty body's site that is a real exposure. The
same logic applies here, and the same care: every logo is served at its
original proportions, unmodified, with no border, no background and no
recolouring.

**How much:** one line from the Secretariat confirming it, and ideally each
authority's own current mark and its brand guidelines, since several of
these files are some years old and an authority that has rebranded will
want the new one.

**What happens if the answer is no, or is slow.** One command clears the
fourteen `logo_path` values and the pages return to the flag-and-acronym
treatment they had before. Nothing else depends on them.

**Recorded:** fourteen rows in `docs/LICENCES.csv`, each carrying
`NOT STATED - CONFIRMATION REQUIRED` as its licence and `Unknown` for
attribution, with the note naming the authority and the source file.

## Alt text for twenty-five photographs inside imported articles

**What we need:** one sentence describing each photograph, or a note that a
photograph is decorative.

**Why we are asking:** twenty-five images sit inside the imported news
articles and project pages. Every one of them carries `alt=""` — Webflow
wrote the empty attribute and nobody filled it in. WCAG 2.1 AA is a
contractual acceptance criterion and an image that carries information a
sighted reader gets is an image that must describe itself.

We have not invented descriptions from filenames. A caption guessed from
`PHOTO-2023-04-27-11-12-41.jpg` would be worse than none, because a screen
reader would announce it as though it were true.

**How much:** a sentence each, or "decorative" where the picture adds
nothing the surrounding text does not already say. Twenty-five in total.

## Four meeting agendas that came across as placeholder text

**What we need:** the real agendas for four public meetings, or
confirmation that they should stay unpublished.

**Why we are asking:** four of the forty-three meetings imported from the
events host carry the events theme's demonstration Latin as their agenda
rather than any CRASA content. The meetings themselves are real: the
titles, the dates and the committees are all genuine.

- **#54** 1st Universal Service and Access Committee Meeting, 10 June 2024
- **#55** Postal Committee, 24 June 2024
- **#56** Human Resource and Development Committee Meeting, 22 July 2024
- **#57** 1st Finance and Audit Committee Meeting, 25 July 2024

**What the platform does in the meantime:** the pages say "Agenda to be
confirmed" in all three languages rather than showing the Latin. The
original text is kept in the database and nothing has been deleted. As soon
as a real agenda replaces it the notice disappears by itself; there is
nothing to switch off.

**This is not the same as the bracketed notes on the privacy and terms
pages.** Those are questions to the Secretariat inside a published
document. This is a statement to the public that a meeting's agenda is not
settled yet.
