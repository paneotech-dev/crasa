# CRASA Platform — Image Sourcing Brief

Working list for downloading and supplying photography. Prepared 8 September 2026.

---

## 0. Before you download anything

**Ask CRASA for their own photography first.** Form 1, Section A, item "Imagery" is still outstanding: *photo and video assets cleared for publication, with credits*. Real photographs of real CRASA meetings, delegates and Secretariat staff are worth more than any stock library, for two reasons. They are unarguably specific to this client, and they are the single strongest defence against the platform reading as generated. Chase this through Sarah Kabahuma this week.

Stock fills the gaps. It should not carry the identity.

**What to avoid, categorically:**

- AI-generated imagery of any kind. Flawless skin, symmetrical faces, glassy eyes, sterile impossible offices. On a regulatory platform this is fatal to credibility.
- Generic corporate stock: handshakes, boardroom tables with laptops, "diverse team pointing at a screen", suited figures against glass towers.
- Gradient washes, abstract geometric shapes or looping animations used *instead of* photographs. Absence of real imagery is itself a tell.
- Anything visibly Western or Northern-hemisphere standing in for Southern Africa. Delegates from fourteen SADC regulators will notice immediately.

**What to prefer:** infrastructure, place, and people doing specific things in recognisably Southern African settings.

## 5. Access and use — people

Used on the homepage pillars, the "why this matters" content, and any universal-access material. This category needs the most care: it is where stock photography most often becomes patronising or fake.


**Need: 5–6.** Look for candid framing, natural light, ordinary clothing, unposed expressions. Reject anything where the subject is smiling directly at the camera holding a device like a product shot.

## 7. Newsroom and publications

News article images are content, not design — CRASA supplies them per article. But you need:

- **3–4 fallback images** for articles published without one, differentiated by sector so the newsroom grid never shows the same placeholder four times.
- **Publication thumbnails** should be generated from the first page of each PDF, not stock. Build that into the upload pipeline; it costs nothing and looks vastly more credible than a stock document photo.

```
document signing regulatory paperwork
annual report publication desk
policy document folder archive
```

## 8. What NOT to source

Do not go looking for section-background textures, abstract gradient meshes, or geometric pattern overlays. The design foundations already establish cream `#F8F6F1` and sand `#E5E0D5` as the quiet surfaces. Decorative backgrounds on top of a working palette is exactly the move that makes a page look assembled rather than designed.

Same for icons. Use a real icon library (Lucide or Phosphor, consistent throughout) — never emoji standing in for icons. Emoji do not inherit CSS colour, break in dark mode, render differently per operating system, and mark a page as unreviewed at a glance.

## 9. Technical specifications

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Hero images | 2400 × 1200 minimum, landscape |
| Sector and context | 1600 × 900 minimum |
| Member country | 1600 × 900 minimum |
| People | 1200 × 800 minimum |
| Format to supply | Original JPG or PNG at full resolution — the platform generates WebP renditions |
| Target delivered size | Under 200 KB per rendition after conversion; heroes under 350 KB |
| Colour | Neutral to slightly warm. Avoid heavily filtered or teal-and-orange graded images — they will fight the green and navy palette |
| Licence | Record source URL, licence type and required attribution for every single file |

**Licence tracking is not optional.** This is an EU-funded platform for a regional intergovernmental body. Every image needs a documented licence. Keep a simple spreadsheet: filename, source, licence, attribution text, date downloaded. It goes into the D5 documentation set.

---

## 10. Sources, in order of preference

1. **CRASA's own archive** — request via Form 1
2. **Wikimedia Commons** — best coverage of Southern African cities and landmarks; check licence per file
3. **Unsplash** — strong for infrastructure and landscape; licence is permissive
4. **Pexels** — good for people and everyday scenes
5. **ITU, World Bank, UN photo libraries** — excellent for genuine telecom infrastructure and development context in Africa, often with clear reuse terms

Avoid AI image generators entirely for this project.
